# Monsters: orcs, trolls, and the hours they keep

Build-order step 4a: the Wilds get teeth. Orcs (fast, light bite) and
trolls (slow, heavy bite) roam as visible, schedule-driven creatures on
**rest/prowl rhythms** — each monster keeps its own seeded hours, attacks
only while prowling, and disengages the moment its prowl ends and walks
home. Palisades, wooden gates, and unfinished stone take damage a
labour-only repair task undoes; workers flee prowling monsters and die if
caught; finished stone and everywhere inside the walls stay absolutely
safe. Watchtowers and schedule display are 4b — this step keeps knowledge
thin and honest.

## Outcome

**What you get:**

- A dangerous world with learnable hours: orcs and trolls rest at
  visible lairs and prowl on their own seeded rhythms — attacking
  palisades, wooden gates, blueprints, and workers caught outside, and
  walking home the moment their prowl ends.
- The concept's promise enforced by systems: inside the walls nothing
  can ever hurt anyone; finished stone is untouchable; a breach is real
  and heals by labour-only repair.
- The threat meter: a ribbon bar that fills toward the nearest
  monster's waking and drains toward its leaving — coarse and fuzzed on
  purpose — plus a per-monster panel with the same rhythm bar on click.
- Real, quiet losses: a caught worker is gone, a grave marks where, and
  the labour pool prices the absence. Old saves migrate into the
  dangerous world deterministically.

**How to verify:**

- Find a lair and watch: the monster sleeps visibly, wakes, walks its
  rounds, and goes home; its panel's rhythm bar and the ribbon meter
  move coarsely with it, never to the exact tick.
- Build a palisade inside a prowler's range: it walks over and bites;
  the segment cracks at thirds; a repairer creeps in between passes; if
  the prowl ends mid-attack the monster leaves the segment standing.
- Let a segment fall: monsters path through the hole, the enclosure
  wash and enclosed count shrink, colonists inside the lost zone flee;
  rebuild the segment and calm returns.
- Send a worker past a prowling orc: they drop everything and run for
  the walls; caught, they die — folk count drops, a grave appears, and
  their carried log is on the ground. A save/load round trip mid-siege
  changes nothing (`npm test` covers the drift proof; lint passes).

## Key decisions

- **Rest/prowl rhythms are the WHEN** (new — the load-bearing choice). A
  monster is either **resting** at its lair (notices nothing, visibly
  dormant) or **prowling** its circuit (notice radius live). Periods and
  offsets are per-monster, seeded at spawn, so each monster keeps
  learnable hours — watch a troll and you know when it wakes. An attack
  ends *only* when the prowl timer does: the monster disengages mid-bite
  and walks home, CONCEPT's "hold until it leaves" made literal. Nothing
  the player does drives one off (avoidance-only stands).
- **Monsters are plain-store entities in `sim/threats/`** (reuses — the
  module ARCHITECTURE already names). `monsters: Monster[]`: id, kind,
  fractional position + prev (the colonist interpolation pattern), lair
  tile, seeded circuit waypoints, **path + step** (stored route state,
  the colonist pattern — no per-tick repathing), phase, phase timer,
  current target. All spawn randomness comes from **one mechanism**: a
  PRNG stream derived from the world seed, used identically by
  generation and the v4 migration, so a migrated v3 save and a fresh
  game on the same seed wake the same monsters. (`MonsterKind` lives in
  the store beside the other enums; the assets/-as-data future noted in
  ARCHITECTURE is deferred with the rest of the content format.)
- **Notice is a radius; the orc/troll split is stats-only** (reuses the
  concept-reframe decision). A prowling monster targets the nearest
  noticeable thing in range — a colonist outside the walls, or a
  damageable wall state — with no kind-specific targeting rules. Orcs
  are fast enough to catch fleeing workers; trolls hit hard enough to
  ruin walls; the split emerges from numbers, as CONCEPT records.
- **Damageable set, per CONCEPT** (extends `sim/walls/` predicates).
  Damageable: `Palisade`, wooden `Gate` (both HP), and every blueprint
  state (one bite destroys sticks; the carried-until-completion rule
  means nothing is lost but the sticks). Immune: `Stone`, `StoneGate` —
  finished stone cannot be touched. A new `isDamageable(state)` predicate
  joins the family.
- **Wall damage + labour-only repair** (new + extends).
  `wallDamageMap: Uint8Array` parallel to `wallMap` — it stores
  **damage** (0 = pristine), so new segments are born clean without a
  write and the max-HP constants stay free tunables forever instead of
  being baked into every save. A bite adds damage; reaching the kind's
  max clears the segment (no refund), marks the chunk, and fires the
  enclosure recompute — the hole is real and danger pours through it.
  `TaskKind.Repair = 9` (append; `TASK_PRIORITY` slots it **directly
  after `BuildWall`** — a breach outranks hauling and chopping, but
  never an active build about to close a ring), generated idempotently
  per damaged built segment, worked by labour alone: the concept's
  counterplay is people, not materials.
- **A lair's ground is never "inside"** (new — closes the stone-trap
  exploit). The enclosure flood seeds from lair tiles as well as the map
  edge, so ground holding a lair can never read as calm however much
  stone surrounds it: walls can *contain* a monster, but the pen stays
  dangerous, colonists in it still flee, and the trapped monster still
  prowls its bounded circuit. Nothing can be built on a lair tile. Both
  promises stay intact — inside means safe, and a monster can never be
  **killed or removed**: contained at most, by stone or by earthworks
  (terraformed cliffs pen a monster exactly as stone does — accepted as
  legitimate siegecraft, priced in labour, permanently hostile ground,
  and a scarred map), and the pen never turns calm.
- **Flee overrides everything, and "inside" means calm** (new). A
  colonist with a prowling monster inside `FLEE_RANGE` **and** not
  standing on an inside tile abandons their task (the standard path:
  reservations release, carried items drop) and runs — a **bounded BFS
  to the nearest inside tile** (never the A\* goal-list search, which is
  O(goals) and would crawl over thousands of inside tiles), or directly
  away from the monster when none is reachable. That includes the whole
  early game: before the first ring closes there is nowhere safe to run
  *to*, and running away is the era's intended texture. Enclosure is the
  *only* wall-awareness flee has — two colonists on outside ground flee
  even with a palisade line between them and the prowler; accepted
  aloud, not an oversight. Colonists on inside tiles ignore monsters
  entirely; when a wall falls, the recompute flips the exposed tiles to
  outside and the calm zone shrinks by itself — no special breach logic.
- **Death is permanent and quiet** (reuses the concept ruling). Caught
  (monster adjacent) means removed: slot vacated, tasks released,
  carried item dropped, the folk and idle readouts simply smaller. No
  mourning mechanics; a small grave prop bakes at the death tile as a
  marker, not a mechanic. Graves never block anything — placement or
  terraform over one clears it silently, and a second death on a tile
  shares the marker.
- **Monsters move by colonist rules, minus every gate** (extends).
  Same A\*, same `MAX_STEP` — cliffs are natural walls, a nice reward
  for keeping outcrops. Gates of both materials never pass monsters;
  colonists pass as today. No monster ever paths through `isBlocking`
  states; they *bite* them instead when noticed.
- **Lairs spawn at generation, denser outward — with no safe zone**
  (new; a deliberate user call). The seeded lair pass places lairs
  anywhere on land, probability rising with radius, trolls biased to the
  outer band — there is **no protected radius**: a lair near the start is
  rare but possible, and the gradient is the only mercy. The single
  exclusion is the spawn clearing itself (~10 tiles), so a new colony
  never shares its courtyard with a monster — spawn sanity, not safety.
  One monster per lair; the lair is a baked prop, so homes are readable
  landmarks.
- **SAVE_VERSION 4** (reuses the ladder). The migration adds
  `wallHpMap` (full HP on every built segment), `graveMap`, and runs
  the lair pass from the save's own seed so old colonies wake up in a
  world that has always had monsters — resting, at first. Fixtures
  v1–v3 keep loading; a v4 fixture joins.
- **Knowledge is approximate by design — and shows it** (reuses the
  doctrine, corrected against CONCEPT). CONCEPT's rule is "schedules show
  approximately; precision is buildable" — so the base game *does* show
  rhythm, coarsely. `sim/know/` exposes each monster's position, kind,
  visible stance, and a **quantized, fuzzed phase estimate**: the true
  timer bucketed into fifths of its phase and offset by a per-monster
  seeded error (±10%), so the display is honest about the rhythm and
  never exact about the minute. Exact timers, circuits, and notice radii
  stay sim-internal; 4b's watchtowers narrow the fuzz. No alarms, no
  toasts — the meter moves, nothing shouts.
- **The threat meter** (new, from the same rule). The ribbon gains a
  five-segment rust meter tracking the colony's most relevant monster —
  the nearest prowling monster **within `THREAT_RANGE` (40 tiles) of**
  the colony anchor (the centroid of its buildings); else the
  soonest-waking monster whose lair sits within that same range; and when
  the range holds nothing at all, the **nearest lair on the map**,
  however far and whatever its monster's phase: the meter is never blank
  while a monster exists, because "time to monsters" was the point. (The
  range applies to *both* near rungs — an unranged prowler rung would
  always hit somewhere on the map and leave the others dead.) While the tracked monster rests the meter **fills** toward
  its waking; while it prowls it **drains** toward its going-home. The
  caption carries the kind *and a coarse time phrase from the same
  fuzzed buckets* — "troll wakes in a day or two", "orc prowling, gone
  within the day", with a "far wilds:" prefix when the tracked lair is
  beyond `THREAT_RANGE` — words, not digits, because the estimate is a
  fifth of a phase wide by design. Clicking a monster opens an
  inspector panel — kind, stance, and the same coarse rhythm bar — the
  per-monster version of watching its rounds. Both meter recipes land in
  `docs/STYLEGUIDE.md` with this change (rust = danger fits the color
  law: never alarm red).

## Goals

- The concept's core promise is now enforced by systems: inside the
  walls nothing can ever hurt you; all risk lives where you chose to put
  people and unfinished walls.
- "When is it dangerous" has a learnable answer per monster — rhythms
  you can watch, windows you can use, and the hold-two-more-hours moment
  when a prowl ends mid-siege.
- Losses are real: a caught worker is gone, and the labour model prices
  the absence without a single mourning mechanic.
- Old saves migrate into the dangerous world deterministically.

## Non-goals

- No combat, no military, no monster deaths — avoidance-only is law
  (CONCEPT). Monsters cannot be killed, trapped permanently, or fought.
- No damage to buildings — monsters bite walls and catch people;
  stockpiles, sawmills, and masons are not targets this step. A breach's
  cost is exposure, not rubble.
- No population inflow — deaths ratchet a five-person colony down with
  no recovery this step. Accepted for 4a and stated plainly: attrition
  to an unplayable colony is possible, and growth/immigration is the
  roadmap's most urgent follow-up once monsters land.
- No watchtowers, no alarms, no route prediction, no *exact* schedule
  display (4b sharpens the fuzz the threat meter and monster panel
  deliberately carry). No sounds.
- No difficulty settings; the outward gradient is the difficulty.

## Design

### Store and migration

```ts
const MonsterKind = { Orc: 0, Troll: 1 } as const;
interface Monster {
  id: number;
  kind: number;
  x: number; y: number; px: number; py: number;  // fractional, lerped
  heading: number;
  lairX: number; lairY: number;
  circuit: number[];        // waypoint tile indices, seeded at spawn
  leg: number;              // current waypoint
  phase: number;            // Rest | Prowl | GoingHome
  phaseTicks: number;       // remaining in this phase
  restTicks: number;        // this monster's seeded period lengths
  prowlTicks: number;
  target: number;           // colonist id, or -1
  targetTile: number;       // wall tile index being bitten, or -1
  biteTicks: number;        // progress toward the next bite
}
```

`wallHpMap` and `graveMap` are `Uint8Array(size²)` beside the other
layers. The v4 migration: full HP stamped on every built wall segment,
empty graves, then the lair pass (below) run from the save's seed via a
derived PRNG stream — byte-deterministic, so the same old save always
wakes the same monsters.

### The rhythm — when a monster is dangerous

Spawn seeds each monster's hours once: `restTicks` drawn from
`REST_BASE` (2 game-days) ± 50%, `prowlTicks` from `PROWL_BASE`
(half a day) ± 50%, initial phase offset random — so lairs don't tick in
unison and each monster's window is its own. The outward gradient
multiplies prowl share: monsters in the outer band prowl up to twice as
long. The circuit is `CIRCUIT_WAYPOINTS` (4) seeded tiles within
`ROAM_RADIUS` (12) of the lair, visited in order while prowling; the
prowl ends wherever it ends and the monster walks home in a `GoingHome`
phase that notices nothing — a leaving monster is already harmless,
which is what makes "hold until it leaves" safe to trust.

Resting monsters notice nothing, chase nothing, bite nothing, and render
dormant at the lair. All danger lives in the prowl.

### Notice, chase, and bite

A **targetless** prowling monster scans `NOTICE_RANGE` (orc 8, troll 6,
Chebyshev) for the nearest noticeable — a colonist in the open on an
*outside* tile, or a tile whose wall state `isDamageable` — and an
acquired target then **holds** until broken: the colonist reaches inside
ground or exceeds `NOTICE_RANGE × 1.5` (hysteresis), the wall tile dies,
or the prowl clock ends — which ends everything, even a one-tile-away
kill; the clock is the law, and that hard stop is what makes "hold until
it leaves" trustworthy. No per-tick nearest-swapping: a chasing orc does
not abandon its victim for a closer fence post. Colonist target: chase
at kind speed (orc 1.3× colonist walk, troll 0.5×); catching means
adjacent — the colonist dies (removal per Key decisions, grave baked).
Wall target: move adjacent and bite on a cadence — orc `ORC_BITE = 1` HP
per second, troll `TROLL_BITE = 4` per 2 s against `PALISADE_HP = 40`,
`GATE_HP = 60` — a troll ruins a palisade segment in ~20 s of contact,
an orc in ~40. Blueprints die in one bite (sticks), and monsters walk
over blueprint tiles harmlessly otherwise — a drawn line partially
erased by a prowl is silent by design, the calm rule applied to bad
news. At max damage: segment clears, no refund, chunk dirty, enclosure
recompute — the existing wall-event path.

Monsters step in the tick **after colonists and before workshops** —
added to `tick.ts`'s documented system order, so a catch always tests
post-move positions and flee always reacts to last tick's monster
positions; determinism keeps its contract. A monster that cannot path —
home, or to its next waypoint — bites the nearest damageable blocker
even while homeward (deterministic desperation); if nothing damageable
blocks it (stone, cliffs), it sleeps where it stands until a path opens,
its rhythm running on.

Damage renders calmly: the segment's baked timber tints darker/ragged at
⅔ and ⅓ HP (re-bake only on threshold crossings, not per bite).

### Flee and the safety boundary

The flee check runs before task work each tick: prowling monster within
`FLEE_RANGE` (6) and self not on an inside tile → abandon (standard
release-and-drop) and the bounded BFS toward inside ground, else
straight away from the monster, re-evaluated per repath. A colonist
*inside a building* is un-noticeable and uncatchable — physically
indoors — wherever the building stands; an outside-the-walls workshop is
therefore a bunker for its slot worker, quirky but harmless, since the
haulers feeding it enjoy no such shelter.
Colonists never flee monsters that are resting, going home, or on the
far side of a wall — `insideMap` *is* the calm zone, and when a segment
falls the recompute shrinks it automatically, which is the whole breach
model: danger pours in because "inside" honestly stopped being inside.

### Repair

Generated per damaged **built** segment (idempotent by tile), claimed
like any task, worked from an adjacent tile at `REPAIR_HP_PER_SECOND`
(4), no materials. Priority sits directly after `BuildWall`. The
under-siege dynamic, stated honestly: a biting monster **camps** its
segment for its whole prowl, and `FLEE_RANGE` keeps the outside ground
beside it lethal — so mid-siege repair works from the **inside face**: a
repairer standing on an inside tile doesn't flee and holds the segment
from behind the line. A closed ring can therefore be held against a
prowl — CONCEPT's outlasting, delivered — while an *unclosed* contested
push cannot be face-repaired against a camper: there the counterplays
are building the other segments faster, writing the segment off, or
falling back — exactly the concept's triad. Between prowls, outside-face
repair is routine.

### Spawning and the gradient

At generation (and once, in the v4 migration): draw candidates by
**seeded weighted sampling with rejection** (the pass's real shape —
earlier "walk the map in seeded order" wording described the same
intent loosely), placing lairs on any land outside the spawn clearing
(~10 tiles)
with probability rising by radius band — no safe radius; a near-centre
lair is rare by the gradient, never impossible — minimum `LAIR_SPACING`
(10) apart, targeting ~`LAIR_TARGET` (25) on the default map. The
**migration's** pass additionally rejects *claimed ground*: tiles
inside the save's enclosure **and the wall tiles themselves** — a wall
tile is never "inside", and a den seeded on the ring would flood the
interior behind it. That enclosure is computed from the save's
`wallMap` **while `monsters` is still empty**: computed after dens
exist, the lair seeds would already include the very flooding this rule
prevents — the order is load-bearing and silent if reversed. Rejected
candidates simply take no lair and sampling continues, so the count
still lands near target. New games have no walls at generation, so the
rule costs nothing there. Kind mix:
orcs anywhere, trolls weighted to the outer third. (CONCEPT's full
gradient also scales speed and ranges outward; 4a scales density and
prowl share only — a deliberate narrowing, left as tuning headroom.)
The lair bakes as a prop (a dark den mound with bones — palette work,
no new render machinery).

### Knowledge, the threat meter, and rendering

`sim/know/` gains `monsters(sim)` — position, kind, heading, stance
(dormant | out) — plus `rhythm(sim, id)`: the coarse phase estimate, the
true timer bucketed into fifths and offset by that monster's seeded
error — the error derived in `know/` from monster id and world seed,
nothing stored, the offset itself quantized so the display is never
sharper than a fifth; that derivation is precisely what 4b's towers
narrow without touching saved state. `threat(sim)` picks the colony's
most relevant monster: a monster **currently biting colony walls**
first; else the nearest prowler **within `THREAT_RANGE` (40)** of the
colony anchor — the building centroid, or the colonist centroid while no
buildings exist; else the soonest-waking lair within the same range;
else the **nearest lair on the map**, whatever its monster's phase — the
meter is empty only on a map with no monsters at all. The caption pairs the kind with a coarse verbal time
from the bucketed estimate ("wakes in a day or two", "gone within the
day"), prefixed "far wilds:" when the tracked lair lies beyond
`THREAT_RANGE`. The pick **holds** until that monster rests, so the bar
never flickers between clocks mid-siege. The ribbon's threat meter renders that: five rust segments
filling toward a waking, draining toward a leaving, captioned with kind
and coarse time. A homeward pick shows an empty bar and "orc heading
home" with no time phrase — `GoingHome` ends on arrival, not a clock.
One honest consequence, stated so nobody reads it as a bug: the time
phrase quantizes to fifths of the phase, so a *long* rest can never read
"any moment now" — a troll one tick from waking still says "wakes within
the day." That is the fuzz doing its job, and narrowing it is exactly
what 4b's towers sell. Clicking a monster opens an
inspector panel (styleguide anatomy: kind as title, ORC/TROLL tag,
stance row, the same five-bucket rhythm bar) — watching one monster's
rounds, as CONCEPT promises. (Picking gains a tile→monster resolution
beside `buildingAtTile`; the monster panel is a second inspector shape.)
The estimate's fuzz is the 4b product boundary: towers will narrow the
buckets, nothing else changes. When the meter recipes land in the
styleguide, its one-meaning color law widens rust's line to "slots,
invalidity, danger — the costly things" rather than gaining a fourth
color.

The movers layer renders orcs as lean, green-tinted folk-scale figures
and trolls as bulky grey half-again taller; resting monsters draw
hunched at the lair. Beyond the meter the HUD adds nothing: no alarm,
no banner — the folk readout shrinking and a grave in the grass are the
game's whole obituary, per the concept's "just losses."

### Determinism

Everything a monster ever does derives from state seeded at spawn plus
the shared tick; no PRNG draws at runtime except through `rngState`.
The golden test gains a scripted encounter: a lair near the colony —
found by *seed selection*, not special spawning — a prowl window
crossing a palisade line, bites, a repair, and a death; byte-identical
across runs, and the save/load drift test runs through it.

## Alternatives considered

- **A protected safe radius around the start** (the spec's original
  default) — rejected by the user: no safe zone; the outward density
  gradient is the only protection, and a rare hard start is accepted as
  part of the game. Only the ~10-tile spawn clearing excludes lairs.

- **Always-dangerous patrol circuits** — danger purely spatial, needs an
  artificial aggro budget to end attacks, and flattens "when is it
  dangerous" into "where is it". Rejected for rest/prowl rhythms.
- **Day/night danger** — maximum legibility, but calendar-driven safety
  was explicitly rejected at concept stage in favour of visible roaming
  threats (see CONCEPT's threat model and the concept-reframe changelog
  entry). Not revisited.
- **Kind-specific targeting rules** (orcs never bite walls, trolls never
  chase) — the concept-reframe entry records the split as *emergent from
  stats*, and stats already produce the intended reads. Rejected.
- **Material-cost repair** — CONCEPT names labour as the counterplay;
  charging logs would double-price a breach. Rejected.

## Implementation strategy

*Not part of the design — a starting point for whoever builds this.*

- **Single agent, Opus 5.** The largest system yet, but one thread: the
  monster entities, tick order, flee (which reaches into colonist
  behavior), wall damage, repair, knowledge, and the threat meter all
  share the store and the determinism contract. Build `sim/threats/`
  with the rhythm and the scripted-encounter golden test first — the
  encounter test pins every rule before rendering exists — then flee and
  death, then damage/repair, then know/meter/panel and the movers
  models; verify against the Outcome section at the end, browser checks
  included.
